How this ranking is built

Thematic ETFs are theme-constrained — they can't rotate out of an expensive theme the way a broad index fund self-cleanses — so a point-in-time look-through valuation actually has teeth. For each fund we value every underlying holding with the same engine behind KashVector's DCF tool and valuation screens (a conservative 5-year discounted cash flow; a dividend or excess-return model for banks, insurers and regulated utilities; an EV/Sales comparison for a small tail the cash-flow models reject), weight each holding's premium or discount by its fund weight, and sum.

Growth input. Where the DCF needs a forward growth rate we use the slower of a holding's multi-year revenue trend and its multi-year free-cash-flow trend (roughly four years of history), rather than a single year's figure. One distorted year — an earnings rebound off a depressed base, say — would otherwise drive a mature company's projection and skew the whole fund's number.

Funds are ordered by that look-through number, descending — the fund whose holdings sit furthest below their combined intrinsic value first. A fund is ranked only when the holdings we can value cover at least 70% of its weight; the rest are listed separately. Crypto funds get no cash-flow valuation at all. Individual holdings whose model output lands outside a sanity band (roughly −200% to +100%) are treated as unvaluable rather than blended in.

Read this as a measurement, not a verdict. This is mechanical model output — not a judgement on any fund, its holdings, or its management, and never a recommendation to buy, hold or sell. The 5-year DCF is deliberately conservative on growth, so a fund reading as a premium is partly the model's own caution, not proof of a bubble. Fast-growing and AI-exposed companies in particular can read as heavily overvalued here because a short-horizon cash-flow model cannot capture a genuine platform shift or growth that is currently showing up as investment rather than free cash flow. A theme can also trade above a conservative intrinsic value for years while compounding. Figures are a point-in-time snapshot as at 2026-09-01 and highly sensitive to assumptions. Frequently asked questions

What does the "vs intrinsic value" number mean?

It is the fund-weighted average premium or discount of the fund's holdings to their own combined intrinsic value. A negative number ("premium") means the holdings collectively trade above what the models say they are worth; a positive number ("discount") means below.

Why is nearly every fund showing a premium right now?

Partly the market, partly the method. The DCF we use is deliberately conservative on growth and applies a hard cap to it, so it systematically values fast-growing companies below their market price. A across-the-board premium says "the market is paying more than a cautious cash-flow model would" — which is normal in a strong market and is not, on its own, a signal that anything is mispriced.

Does a premium mean I should sell?

No. Nothing on this page is advice, and it is never a buy/sell/hold call. A theme can trade above a conservative intrinsic value for a very long time while still compounding. Treat the number as one input — a measure of how much optimism is priced in — not a timing signal.

Why do AI and big-tech names read as so overvalued?

A 5-year discounted cash flow can't price a genuine platform shift, and it penalises companies whose growth currently shows up as capital investment rather than free cash flow. Names like the large semiconductor and cloud businesses will often read as steep premiums here for that reason. It reflects the model's known limitation as much as the stock's price.

What is "coverage" and why does it matter?

The share of a fund's weight we could actually put a valuation on. Some holdings can't be valued (no usable cash-flow data, a currency mismatch we couldn't resolve, or a model output outside the sanity band). The look-through number is computed over the covered portion only, so a fund at 75% coverage tells you less than one at 100%. Below 70% we don't publish a headline figure at all.

How often is this updated?

Monthly. Holdings come from each issuer's published full-holdings file; prices and fundamentals are a monthly snapshot from Yahoo Finance, not a live feed.

Why only thematic ETFs, not VAS or IVV?

Broad market-cap index funds reconstitute — they drop what gets expensive — so a look-through verdict on them has little predictive value (one can read "overvalued" for a decade while compounding). Thematic funds are locked to their theme, which is what makes the measurement meaningful.

Which valuation models do you use?

A conservative 5-year discounted cash flow for most holdings; a dividend-discount or excess-return model for banks, insurers and regulated utilities; and an EV/Sales peer comparison for a small tail of companies the cash-flow models reject. The same engine powers KashVector's DCF tool and its undervalued screens.
